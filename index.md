## 🎛️ The Single Modifier

The heart of SiriGlass is one short line:

`.siriGlass($state)`

You attach it to a view, hand it a state value, and the effect takes care of itself. When the state changes, the Liquid Glass drop appears, pours, and settles. When your voice rises, the glow follows. When the state returns to rest, the glass melts back into the Dynamic Island.

There is nothing to initialize, no delegate to register, no animation curve to tune. The modifier is the whole API, and that is intentional — the goal is a single, memorable action that any SwiftUI developer can recall instantly.

## 🧠 How It Works, In Plain Language

Think of the Dynamic Island as a small glass bottle hanging at the top of the screen. SiriGlass fills that bottle with liquid glass. When something triggers the effect, the bottle tips and the glass pours out, spreading into a rounded blob that hovers on the screen. A light source inside the glass responds to sound — quiet moments produce a soft shimmer, louder moments produce a bright pulse.

When the moment passes, the liquid is pulled back into the bottle and the screen returns to normal. The whole sequence is animated frame by frame, which is why it looks smooth rather than choppy.
